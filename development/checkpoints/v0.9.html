Checkpoint V0.9: how reliable is the verifier itself?

Status: working development record, 2026-11-27. Index page for an original checkpoint. Original files: README, page, handoff, code.

In plain words

A Buddy check is only useful if the check itself does not damage the data. V0.9 asked how often the checking circuit lets damage through unnoticed, and whether cleverer circuits do better. The answer is yes at first order, but not completely, and not always.

Question

How reliable is Buddy's own extraction of the checks, which established method should be the baseline, and what should Buddy's configurations be before anything is added to its responsibilities?

Model and assumptions

Generic Pauli-noise qubits with ideal Clifford gates. No photon loss, no probabilistic two-photon gates, no hardware. The fault probability p is "per circuit location" and is a parameter. Three fault models: M1 (ancilla-only, the V0.8 regression), M2 (every Pauli on the touched qubits of each gate, plus measurement flips), M3 (M2 plus idle faults on live qubits and incoming data faults). All ASSUMED. Exact distributions come from exact propagation of every single fault and every pair of faults, with independent location faults convolved exactly.

Results DERIVED

  • Flagged extraction and verified-cat extraction of the joint checks have zero first-order silent logical damage under M2 and M3 (196 single faults under M2, 460 under M3). The conventional joint round has 4 silent cases of 128, and the conventional local round 8 of 68.
  • Second-order damage remains: second-order coefficient 2.320 (flagged) versus 2.427 (conventional joint) and 1.244 (local) under M2.
  • A concurrency rule: an in-code rotation can be inserted after k data couplings of a check exactly when its generator commutes with the part of the check already coupled. Of 18 weight-2 joint generators, interior windows exist for 34 of 54 (generator, slot) pairs, and 1,638 state-vector simulations confirmed the rule. This is a statement about compatible one-rotation interleaving with no inserted faults, not about simultaneous physical execution.
  • Reference preparations can establish operational assurance for a constant apparatus error: to see at least one click with 95% confidence, a constant controlled-phase angle error of 1.00 rad needs 12 reference runs, 0.30 rad 133, 0.10 rad 1,198, 0.03 rad 13,314 and 0.01 rad 119,829 (the click probability is exactly sin²(d/2)). They say nothing about an individual unknown state, and a reference set needs non-eigenstates of every error generator it must expose.

Negative results

  • Flagging does not remove silent damage: it moves the dominant term from p to *p*².
  • The flagged round does not always win: under M3 the longer flagged circuit loses to the conventional joint round above p ≈ 1.1e-3 per location.
  • The cooperative variant tested (a two-cat-qubit round coupling both axes of one photon to each cat qubit) stays non-fault-tolerant even with a verified cat: 0.8 silent per unit p under M2.
  • Extra machinery increases the number of fault cases that leave some damage on the data (3.2, 6.9, 9.1, 16.8 per unit p for local, joint, flagged, verified cat) and the rejection rate.
  • The evidence supports the possibility that Buddy has several configurations selected by IQA, not that they are worth their combined complexity.

Corrections

Erratum E4 corrects V0.8's wording about conversion: the transported checks exist at every step and read +1 on converted logical states; what stops being valid is the complete original check set. See Errata. During the build I also fixed an inverted Pareto selection and a raw-weight versus stabilizer-coset-weight misreport, and withdrew an over-stated concurrency assertion (documented in the original page).

Limits

Generic qubits, uniform noise, ideal Clifford gates; no loss, no probabilistic gates, no hardware; literature at title or abstract level (UNVERIFIED). Nothing independently reproduced.

Reproduction
