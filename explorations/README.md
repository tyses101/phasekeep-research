# Exploration pack S: exact analyses on top of checkpoint V1.0

**Status: exploratory, post-V1.0, 2026-11-27. Author-side. Not a checkpoint, not V1.1, not a measurement.** Nothing here changes any earlier file. Every script runs with `python3 -I` and prints `ALL PASS` only if all of its own checks pass; each folder holds its recorded execution. All execution is by the same author (Claude) that wrote the scripts, so it is **not independent reproduction** ([Reproducibility](../docs/development/reproducibility.md)).

This pack answers the project owner's open-ended request of 2026-11-27 ("make educated and provable speculation based on all the data we have and anything we might have overlooked"). It was done without separate V1.1 authorization, so it is labelled exploratory: the owner decides whether any of it is adopted into the V1.1 roadmap, and the roadmap's own ground rules still apply (earlier checkpoints preserved; negative results reported; the local geometry kept as the baseline; no hardware advantage claim without a measurement).

Evidence tags as elsewhere in the repository: **DERIVED** (exact on the stated model), **ASSUMED** (a rule or rate I chose and can name), **PROPOSED**, **UNVERIFIED**, **MEASURED** (none), **BLOCKED**.

Standard library plus `numpy` (S01, S03 projector check, S05, S07 to S10; S06 and S11 need only the standard library and the other scripts; S12 to S22 need numpy; S14 takes about two minutes; S15 to S22 load the state-vector runner of S05 and, from S16 on, each other, by file path). Python 3.13, numpy 2.5.3 were used.

## Cross-cutting findings

**1. The answer depends on what a failed gate does, and that is still open (open problem 1).** The exploration does not resolve it; it turns it into a table (below) that a gate-level model or measurement could fill in. The semantics tested are *erasure*: a heralded failure destroys the qubits it acts on (either just those axes, "EraseAxis", or their whole photons, "ErasePhoton"). Erasure is the conservative end of the "destroys the qubits" family: a failure that merely measures the qubits in a known basis is a post-processing of an erasure, so a harmless erasure is also harmless there (argued, not tested). A benign failure (the gate does nothing and is retried) is V1.0's other case and is not affected by anything here.

**2. Two axes per photon is the structural weak point.** It appears in four independent places: the unheralded-loss floor (S02), every data-touching failure being fatal under photon erasure (S07), the conversion gaining nothing from clean axes (S08), and the ancilla co-hosting results (S09). The one geometry where the V1.0 joint code has photon-level distance 2 is one axis per photon (twice the photons). Codes that survive the loss of a 2-axis photon exist (S03) but cost more check weight (S10 puts a number on it).

**3. Extraction design matters for failure recoverability even though it does not matter for the loss floor.** S02 shows the unheralded-loss floor is identical for all joint designs; S07 and S09 show that whether a heralded failure leaves the information intact depends strongly on the design (flag and cat protect only while the ancilla qubits are on separate photons).

**4. Offline preparation helps only the verified-cat round (S04), and conversion is the more fragile primitive (S07, S08).**

**5. "Harmless failure" is a ceiling, and the price of recovery decides whether it is an advantage at all (S12).** S07's P = 1 for the flagged and cat rounds is the limit of free recovery. Charging a recovery that needs m inter-photon gates, the joint round beats the local round only above a crossover per-gate success (0.86 at m = 3, 0.93 at m = 8 for the cat round), and the conventional joint round never does. S13 constructs a 3-gate recovery (teleportation into an offline-prepared resource, which costs 8 extra photons and its own verification) and proves an in-place recovery needs at least 5, so even the best recovery known here leaves the ancilla-check joint advantage confined to s above about 0.86. S14 shows the exposure is not intrinsic to the joint code: verifying by teleportation gives it the local round's 4 data-touching gates, and then the joint code is ahead of the local one at every s on the heralded-completion metric (silent faults and the resource's own faults are evaluated in finding 6). Below the crossover the local round, which simply loses the information, completes more often. This agrees in direction with the closed-form Model B crossover (s = 0.577) but is not the same quantity and the numbers are not comparable.

**6. The heralded metric and the silent-fault metric rank S14's teleportation round differently, and neither alone decides (S14 to S17, S20).** On heralded completion with recovery charged the teleportation round leads (S14). On exact silent probability at p = 1e-3 it sits between two groups even with a perfect resource: 14 to 27 times below the local and conventional joint rounds, but 2 to 8.5 times above the verified-cat and flagged joint rounds (S20). With S14's resource as written it is no better than the conventional joint round (hook faults in the weight-4 checks give it the same first-order silent term), and none of the five resource designs tried brings the second-order term below 19.68 against 2.32 (flagged) and 9.90 (cat) (S17). Readout flips about 10 times rarer than gate faults would put a perfect-resource round level with the flagged round (S16, S20); that is a condition on a platform, not a finding about one. The two axes are shown side by side below and are not combined, as the roadmap's ground rule requires.

**7. V0.9's idle-fault numbers and V1.0's bias, readout and loss tables reproduce under a second engine (S18, S19)**, and the exact model adds the X-bias crossing (b = 26 to 28 for the conventional and flagged joint rounds, 6.9 for the cat) and the exact flagged-versus-conventional crossing in p (1.05e-3; V0.9's printed 1.12e-3 is a scan-grid point). V1.0's repeated-round, Pauli-class, capability and Pareto sections (S21) also reproduce, and its amplification table is within 1.4 % of the exact binomial minimum (up to 2.3 % in the saving factor). This is the author's own second implementation, so open problem 15 stays open.

**8. The flagged joint round is the round most exposed to coherent noise (S22).** With rotations of equal angle and sign on every location, its leading silent coefficient is 2 to 13.8 times the Pauli-twirled one (7.9 under Z, 13.8 under X, 9.8 under a tilted axis), against 1 to 3 for the conventional joint and local rounds, and its advantage over the conventional joint round falls from 25 to 71 times to 2.7 to 8.1 times at s = 1e-3. The ranking among the joint rounds stays flagged/cat below conventional, but the flagged-versus-cat order depends on the noise being Pauli. The same-sign assumption is the extreme end: averaged over random signs the coefficient is the twirled one. V0.9's and V1.0's rankings therefore rest on the Pauli-twirl assumption (open problem 18), and S22 puts numbers on how far a systematic coherent error can move them.

"Fatal" means the logical information is not recoverable from what survives at that instant. It is an **upper bound on usable outcomes**: harmless failures still erase qubits that must be rebuilt with further inter-photon gates (S12 charges for this under a stated process), and unheralded loss (S02) is a separate floor that none of this touches. Fatal points are not probabilities; the per-round loss probability is Σ over fatal gates of s^(i−1)(1 − s) (printed in S09). The teleportation row counts only the four online data-touching gates and takes the offline resource as correct; S15 to S20 evaluate that resource's faults on the silent metric (finding 6 and the two-axes table above).

## Mistakes and retractions in this pack

These statements of mine were wrong or over-stated and were caught by the scripts or by review; each is kept visible in the folder's `run-history.txt` or here.

1. **S02**: the first comparison with V1.0's printed slopes failed a 5 % tolerance (cat 5.4 %) because V1.0's column is conditioned on no heralded ancilla loss; fixed by dividing by (1 − q)^(ancilla locations), tolerance 3 %. The remaining 2.5 % is attributed to loss × gate-fault cross terms, **which I did not compute**. A vacuous `or True` check was removed.
2. **S04**: hard-coded unit counts were off by one (145/4713 versus 146/4714); replaced by checking the defining property. A circular check was removed.
3. **S05**: my first parse took V0.9's M3 table instead of M2; random cat readouts crashed the simulator until branches were enumerated.
4. **S06**: the "maximum = n − 1" pattern was falsified at n = 5 (above).
5. **S07**: an earlier single model concluded "extraction design does not matter"; the exact full-state version contradicts that, so both are kept (A and B) and the design-independence statement is limited to B. My first F4 claim ("no failure is recoverable at two axes per photon") was over-stated and my own check failed. The earlier draft was reported to the owner only as a superseded model.
6. **S08**: I claimed no conversion circuit has fewer than two fatal points; the exact search found a 7-gate counterexample (above). A vacuous sub-condition was removed from the check that describes it.
7. **S09**: I claimed the verified cat also degrades at K = 2; it does not (only at K = 5).
8. **S10**: two script bugs (wrong slice of V1.0's op list; phase correction for Y factors) and an over-broad label in the negative control.
9. **Review pass over the whole pack**: four checks that could not fail (S07 G0, S10 E2b and E2c, S11 P2d) were replaced by real conditions; S02's "c1 > 0" checks were made exact (c1 = 1, c2 = 3); a README threshold ("q ≥ 4.8 p") contradicted V1.0's own table and was removed; the README's statement that the joint round never beats the local one for s < 1 held only for the conventional round; and a description of S01 as "two ququarts' worth" was wrong.
10. **S12**: my first "independent" exact check was the closed form's own equations restated; replaced by an exhaustive enumeration of all gate outcomes. A sentence calling m = 3 "the smallest value I could argue is plausible" was an unsupported lower bound; it is now described as illustrative only.
11. **S14**: my first draft expected 12 preparation gates for the local code's resource (it is 8), I wrote the local code's detected error types backwards before the run (its Z-parity checks catch X and Y, not Z), and an early version of the recoverability test built a 2^13 by 2^13 matrix per block (a memory blow-up, replaced by a reduction to the joint support).
12. **S15 to S17 (scripts)**: my first independent cross-check of the ideal-resource second-order coefficient gave 92/5 because I treated the measurement-record flips as the physical error pattern (the simulation's 492/25 was right; replaced by a Pauli-frame count and a closed form); a check that could not fail (F5b) was replaced, which forced a second 30-minute run; a prototype file was truncated by my own slicing; S17's first run used a brute-force null-space routine that is infeasible at 32 record bits and wasted about 19 CPU-minutes before I replaced it with a tested Gauss–Jordan version (see the folders' `run-history.txt`).
13. **S15, S16, S17 (reading), corrected by S20**: I compared second-order coefficients only, and wrote that a perfect resource is "15.8 times the local round and 8.1 times the conventional joint round". The local and conventional joint rounds have a first-order silent term that dominates at every realistic p; the exact silent probabilities (S20) show the perfect-resource teleportation round **below** both at p = 1e-3 (27× and 14×) and above only the flagged and cat rounds. My interim status reports to the owner may have carried the same framing; I corrected it in a later report once S20's exact numbers existed. The c2 comparison stands against the flagged and cat rounds, whose first-order term is 0.
14. **S19**: I expected V0.9's printed "M3 crossover … p = 1.12e-03" to be the exact crossing and my first check failed (exact 1.050e-3). V0.9's code reports the first point of a 12 %-step scan grid at which the conventional round is lower, so the printed number is a grid point and its stated interval is correct; the check now reproduces the grid definition and reports the exact crossing alongside.
15. **S21 (my first amplification comparison)**: my first run reported V1.0's amplification run counts as up to 3.4 % off the exact binomial answer. That was my own stepped search, not V1.0: the set of run counts that meet both exact tails is a sawtooth, not an interval, and I had compared V1.0 against a feasible count that was not the minimum. The script now finds the true minimum (and a brute-force scan confirms it); V1.0's counts are within 1.4 % above it, plus one best-N count 2.2 % below. Retracted; see `s21-v10-remaining-sections/run-history.txt`.
16. **S22 (my checks)**: my first full run failed one check because I had guessed the number of sign-average cases (it is 7, not 8), and my second run's last check asserted True and so could not fail; I replaced it and two others with assertions on the computed relations, which are regression checks written after seeing the run, not predictions (see `s22-coherent-rotations/run-history.txt`). ## Not claimed

No experimental hardware validation, no demonstrated reduction of physical cost, no quantum or efficiency advantage, no independent reproduction, no statement about any real device's failure behaviour, noise bias or readout-to-gate error ratio. Every probability is a calculation under assumed rates or semantics. The failure semantics (erasure of axes or photons), the hosting rules, the packing of ancilla qubits and the intra-photon-gates-are-ideal rule are assumptions and are named wherever they are used. Stabilizer-basis and Clifford-circuit results say nothing about non-stabilizer bases, continuous-variable or other high-dimensional photonic encodings.
