S30 run history (author-side; all runs by the same author that wrote the script)

First draft run (killed by me after 11 minutes, output partly read): H0, H1 and H4a passed, H2b and H3 FAILED, and the break-even scan was far too slow (a Python loop to 100000 layers per cell).  What the failures were:
  - H2b: M3 and M4 showed silent coefficient 0 and every fault 'detected'.  The helper that tests whether the two decode syndrome qubits are |0> removed one axis and then indexed the second with the wrong sign of the offset, so it tested the wrong qubit.  Fixed by testing with a basis-index mask.
    The M2 gate-count comparison was unaffected; every number that depended on 'detected' changed (M1's silent coefficient went from 32/5 to 33/5, M3's from 0 to 289/15).
  - H3: the 'decode with a wrong gate order' control gave fidelity 1.000.  I had reordered gates that commute, so the circuit was the same.  Replaced by a genuinely wrong operation that keeps the syndrome clean (an unintended CZ between the data qubits after the decode).
  - a vacuous 'or True' on the check that ranks the mechanisms by silent coefficient was removed (the ordering is now only printed, and the note says the coefficients are not comparable across mechanisms without comparing where the information ends up).
  - the break-even scan is now vectorised with a closed-form per-qubit distribution (Walsh-Hadamard on Z2^2), cross-checked against the explicit convolution and against S29's recorded Z2c value (silent / q^2 = 2.000).
Second run: FAILED H4d only.  The check asserted that the exact first crossing lies within 12 % of the leading-order estimate sqrt(c1 / (c2 rho_p^2 p)); it was 15 % off at rho_p = 3 and p = 1e-3.  I replaced the tolerance (which I had chosen before seeing the values) by what the estimate actually is: an asymptote.  The check now requires the deviation to fall monotonically
  with p (+12.1 %, +3.5 %, +1.0 %, +0.3 % at p = 1e-3 ... 1e-6) and to be below 2 % at p = 1e-6.  That is a property of the derivation, not a tuned threshold, but I wrote the pass condition after seeing the first values.
Before recording I also made these changes (none changed a number):
  - break-even windows were scanned to 200000 layers; at that length the linear cross term for M5 and saturation make them meaningless, so the scan is capped at 3000 and the text says that a window ending at 3000 is cut by the scan range;
  - the lower-bound labels said 'data on photon B' where the data are on photon A (the computed bounds were right: 2, 1, 2, 2);
  - dead variables and an unused walrus removed; the T-gate row of H5 now says that the first-order change is an entanglement-fidelity statement (the Choi fidelity depends only on the trace of the error operator).
  - H6 (loss exposure by hosting) added after the second run.
Independent evidence in this script: H0b (targets built separately from the circuits: two Bell pairs, the relabelled source state, and five commuting independent stabilizer expectation values), H1 (exact Schmidt ranks from SVD of the state vectors), H3 (negative controls pass every structure test and fail only the reference fidelity), H4a (second implementation of the idle model against S29), H4c (exhaustive double faults: 20 + 4 locations).
Not independent: the encoder and decoder are one circuit and its inverse (so H0 tests the transfer, not the encoder: that the input is a code state is checked by the stabilizers), and the fault model is the one used by every earlier script.
Final run: ALL PASS (see execution-record.txt).

CORRECTION after the first commit (851c70f), found while designing S31 (found by me, before anyone had used the numbers):
  The first version's round trips reused the source qubits left over after the forward move, and ended with the exact inverse of the forward circuit.  A fault in the forward part was then partly UNCOMPUTED by the return path (the total circuit with a fault after A, B-forward and its inverse is A^-1 P A: the
  gates after the fault cancel), and a fault on the preparation of a destination qubit was harmless for that reason.  That is not available when the source register is discarded or reconfigured while the information is parked (the scenario under study).  Corrected: after a decode the two syndrome
  qubits are read out before they are discarded (a 1 is a detection at decode time), the source qubits are reset, and all four are freshly prepared at return (four charged preparation locations).  Effect on the printed numbers (all unbiased, M2 weights):
      M1 round-trip first-order silent coefficient of gates and preparations 9.2 -> 13.2 (the forward destination preparations, 2, and the fresh data qubits, 2, are silent; the CNOT part 46/5 is unchanged);
      M5 exact second-order gate coefficient 20.48 -> 70.08 (3.4 times), gate-park 12.8 -> 20.8 per parked layer; M5 first-order silent still 0;
      M6x first-order 10.93 -> 14.93;
      window starts (p = 1e-3, rho_m = 0, rho_p = 1): M1 76 -> 93 layers, M5 4 -> 6 layers, M6x 84 -> 100 layers; X3 under b = 100: window 84-972 -> 101-933.
  The qualitative statements are unchanged (M5 never wins at equal idle rate; M1 pays a first-order window; X3 needs bias), but every number that depends on the round-trip coefficients moved, and the earlier README text must not be quoted.
Final run after the correction: ALL PASS (see execution-record.txt).
