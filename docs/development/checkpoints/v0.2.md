# Checkpoint V0.2: logical gates against geometry, targeted verification

**Status: working development record, 2026-11-27. Index page for an original checkpoint.** Original files: [README](../../../checkpoints/v0.2/README.md), [page](../../../checkpoints/v0.2/docs/tetragon-map-v0.2.md), [handoff](../../../checkpoints/v0.2/CODEX_HANDOFF.md), [code](../../../checkpoints/v0.2/code/tetragon_map.py).

## Question

Does a free choice of output geometry change what it costs to carry out a logical gate, and how can a diagonal encoding be verified with few settings?

## Model and assumptions

Ideal noise-free model, nine parts. Gates X, Z, S and H on the 12 two-vertex encodings (8 edge, 4 diagonal).

## Results [DERIVED]

- Only the logical flip X gains from a free output geometry, by relabelling the encoding (the same idea as Pauli-frame tracking, which is established and UNVERIFIED here from memory).
- For these four gates the edge encoding is never more expensive than the diagonal one; Z and S are single-axis in both. The diagonal encoding is valuable for other reasons: either axis can apply a phase gate, and a single physical flip is detectable by parity.
- Targeted verification of the diagonal encoding needs three local settings instead of nine for full two-qubit tomography, all destructive and without an entangling gate.
- If no leaked events are seen in n shots, leakage is below about 3/n at 95% confidence. This verifies the apparatus on test photons, not one particular computational photon.

## Negative or sobering results

For one logical qubit, adaptive switching cannot save operations against the fusion baseline: a sequence of single-qubit gates on an edge encoding fuses into one unitary on one axis. Geometry could still matter where fusion is blocked, where the carrier axis is lossy, for verification, and in larger registers (not tested). A nondestructive parity measurement is the verification we want, and a photonic implementation is not established.
