# 14. Scientific framing

[← Pending research](13_pending.md) · [Back to contents](00_contents.md)

> **In plain words:** this is what we claim, and what we don't.

[ASSUMED] Scope: numerical statements apply only to the stated model, sequence and sampled parameters. They are not hardware guarantees.

## Central question
Can physically embedded multidimensional quantum registers exploit native geometry, energetic confinement and symmetry-based control to execute and preserve quantum information more efficiently than conventional implementations?

## What the work supports
Mathematical derivations, and simulated behaviour **under specified ideal models**.

## What it does **not** establish
- a fundamentally new quantum computational primitive (the register is a known structure: chapter 2)
- quantum computational advantage
- superior physical resource efficiency
- fault-tolerant quantum computation
- a functioning quantum memory
- any experimental hardware performance
- originality of the individual mechanisms

## Established fields this work draws on (to be cited, not claimed)
- digital-analog quantum computing
- unary (one-hot) qudit encodings
- synthetic dimensions
- single-excitation subspaces
- stabilizer codes
- dynamical decoupling and filter functions
- composite pulses and optimal control
- noise-aware compilation
- decoherence-free subspaces and clock states
- erasure conversion

See [sources](../03_reference/sources.md).

## Where a contribution might lie
- **Physical embedding:** unary rings and hypercubes with gap protection.
- **Architectural integration:** register, addressing, instructions and assurance designed as one system.
- **Control strategy:** HOLD / ROTATE / COUPLE, plus asymmetric-hopping frequency planning.
- **Testable relationships:** the εα tolerance rule, the drift-balance condition and the ∫g² envelope-balance condition.

All of these need a prior-art review before any novelty is asserted.
