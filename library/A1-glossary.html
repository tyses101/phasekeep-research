Glossary

Every term in one place

Every term in one place. Terms in bold are defined where they first matter; the volume number is given in brackets.

Project terms

TermMeaning
EA (Encoding Axis, plural Axies)A physical property of light chosen to carry a label, with distinguishable values. Vol. 3
CT (Controlled Transformation)A physical operation we can set and repeat that changes the encoded information. "Controlled" means adjustable, not "conditioned on another qubit"; conditional operations are one kind. Vol. 5
GEA (Geometric Encoding Axies)Reading the states of a register as the corners of a square, cube or hypercube. Vol. 4
Buddy SystemThe proposed way to manage preparation, assistance, verification, preservation and continuation using helper (ancillary) photons. Vol. 7
Buddy CheckA test that answers PASS, FAIL or UNKNOWN, based only on demonstrated evidence. Vol. 7
Tetragon (PK-Q4)The four-address register: H/V polarization × twist −1/+1. Vol. 3
PK-MThe proposed four-mode atomic-vapor memory and its certification test. Vol. 8
PK-EThe proposed prepared-instruction execution scheme. Vol. 10
RegisterAn information-carrying system with a defined set of states. Binary, Structured and Adaptive forms are described. Vol. 10
Adaptive RegisterA register whose mapping or encoding could be chosen or reconfigured within supported capabilities. Proposed only. Vol. 10
AddressOne of the basis states of a register, for example 01 on the Tetragon. Vol. 3
ClosureWhether an operation keeps the photon inside the chosen set of states. Vol. 5
Axis family namesQubit, Tetragon, Cubion, Tesseron, Penteron, Hexeron, Heptaron, Octeron, Ennearon, Decaron, Aetheron, Hyperion: registers of 1 to 12 binary axes. Labels only. Vol. 4
PQGS (Photonic Quantum Geometric Superpositions)A descriptive term for coherent photonic superpositions organized in a geometrically structured encoding space, especially non-separable ones spanning several EAs. Research terminology inside GEA, not a pillar. "Geometric" does not mean geometric phase. Vol. 4
State Space (SS) / State Space Environment (SSE)The mathematical space of all available quantum states; and the physical resources and control that make it accessible and useful. Vol. 10
Occupation / connectivity / correlationThe three things an environment can change during a computation: which addresses carry amplitude, which can be coherently coupled, and how the encoded properties are related. Vol. 10
Available / active / operational geometryThe addresses supported; the addresses taking part in the current state; the relationships that the available operations can manipulate. Vol. 10
SubspaceThe set of states that can be built from a chosen group of addresses; a line gives a two-dimensional subspace and a square a four-dimensional one. Vol. 4
SupportThe set of addresses that have non-zero amplitude. Not the same as the state, since phases and correlations are also needed. Vol. 10
Address geometry / state-space geometry / optical-mode geometry / execution geometryThe four kinds of geometry: mathematical, mathematical, physical, physical. Vol. 4
TimeThe machine's clock. Not an EA in the current design. Vol. 3

Light and quantum terms

TermMeaning
PhotonThe smallest packet of light. Vol. 1
Polarization (H, V)The direction in which the light's electric field swings. Vol. 1
OAM (orbital angular momentum), twist ℓA spiral structure of the wavefront; ℓ is a whole number. Phase factor exp(iℓφ). Vol. 1
Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) modeThe common family of twisted beams. Vol. 1
WaveplateA crystal slice that changes polarization. Vol. 1
Q-plateA device that ties polarization to twist. Not closed on {−1, +1} as is. Vol. 5
Dove prismA prism that rotates an image; used for a twist-dependent phase. Vol. 5
SorterA device that sends different twists to different places. Vol. 4
Beam splitter / PBSSplits light by amplitude, or by polarization. Vol. 5
SuperpositionA state that is a blend of several basis states. Vol. 2
AmplitudeThe complex number multiplying a basis state; its squared size is a probability. Vol. 2
Relative phaseThe angle between amplitudes; observable only through interference. Vol. 2
No-cloning theoremAn unknown quantum state cannot be copied perfectly. Vol. 2
FidelityA score from 0 to 1 for how close a state or process is to a target. Always say which definition. Vol. 2
Process fidelityFidelity of an operation rather than of one state. Vol. 6
Coherent stateThe quantum state of ordinary laser light; n̄ is its mean photon number. Vol. 8
Thermal noise (v)Added unwanted light per mode in the memory model. Vol. 8
Efficiency (η)Overall fraction of light that comes back through the chain. Vol. 8
IsolationSelecting a subspace by filtering or projecting. Not reversible; the rest of the state is lost. Vol. 4
High-dimensional entanglementEntanglement between systems with more than two levels each. The project's adopted scientific foundation; the first target is two entangled Tetragons (16 joint states, up to 2 ebits). Vol. 6
EbitUnit of entanglement: one maximally entangled pair of two-level systems. A pure state with r equal Schmidt weights holds log2 r ebits. Vol. 6
HyperentanglementEntanglement in several properties of the same two photons at once, for example polarization and twist. Vol. 6
Independent, coupled, entangling operationThree kinds of operation on a register with two axes: one separate operation per axis; one that needs coordinated control of both axes but never entangles a product input (a SWAP of the axes); one that can turn a product input into an entangled state (a controlled phase). Vol. 6
Correlation patternWhich address of photon B goes with which address of photon A in an entangled pair, such as corresponding or cyclic. Vol. 6
QuditA quantum system with d levels instead of 2. The Tetragon is a four-level qudit. Vol. 13
FusionAn entangling measurement used in fusion-based quantum computation. Vol. 13
Hilbert spaceThe set of all possible states of a system. Vol. 2
Separable / non-separableA state is separable if it can be written as one polarization state times one twist state. Otherwise it is non-separable. Vol. 4
Schmidt weights, Schmidt rankThe weights describing how strongly two parts of a state are tied together; rank is how many are non-zero. Separable states have rank 1. Vol. 4
Vector vortex beamA beam whose polarization varies around the beam axis. Non-separable polarization-twist states give such beams. Vol. 4
Geometric phaseThe Berry or Pancharatnam-Berry phase. A different idea from "geometric" in GEA. Vol. 4
TomographyReconstructing a state or process from many measurements. Vol. 11

Gates and heralding

TermMeaning
SUMControlled-SUM: the pair (j, k) becomes (j, (j+k) mod 4). Vol. 6
CP, CPFControlled-phase operation, and the controlled-phase-flip that flips the sign of one state. Vol. 6
KickbackA target prepared in a special state passes a phase back to the control. Vol. 6
Operator Schmidt rankHow many product terms an operation needs when written as a sum of one-operation-per-system terms. Rank 1 means it factors. A higher rank means it does not factor, which is a mathematical statement and not a measure of physical interaction strength or of entangling power. In Volume 6 it gives a floor on how many interaction gates are needed under a stated gate model; the floor is not always tight and does not predict optical component count. Vol. 6
Ancillary (auxiliary) photonA helper photon that is measured instead of kept. Vol. 7
HeraldA detector signal meant to announce success. Vol. 7
Post-selectionKeeping only the runs that look right. Vol. 6
Occupancy-validExactly one photon in each computational output register. Not state fidelity. Vol. 7
False heraldThe herald fires but the output is not occupancy-valid. Vol. 7
Herald probability / reliabilityHow often it fires; how often it is right when it fires. Vol. 7
Output fidelity, availability, continuation yieldThe other three Buddy measures. Vol. 7
PNR detectorPhoton-number-resolving detector. Vol. 7
Monte CarloEstimating by many random trials. Vol. 8

Earlier project terms

These come from earlier PhaseKeep material and review discussions, and were recovered for this edition. They are not part of the adopted EA/CT vocabulary, and the definitions below should be confirmed by the project owner PROPOSED.

TermMeaning
Quantum threadingThe project's earlier name for coordinating preparation, routing, storage and execution.
DDQT (Demand-Driven Quantum Threading)Confirmed project term. One operating mode of quantum threading, with scheduling that can be classical. Flow-based: prepare, select successful resources, synchronize and execute without a persistent instruction bank. Inventory-based: prepare ahead of demand, store, request and retrieve.
PKC (PhaseKeep Conductor)Confirmed project term. The classical supervisory system that handles scheduling, synchronization, resource management and optimization. It was proposed as a rename to avoid "PIC", which in photonics means photonic integrated circuit.
PK-BS (PhaseKeep Buddy System)The project identifier for the Buddy System. Vol. 7
AIS (Autonomous Instruction Streaming)Confirmed project term. Three levels. AIS-1: classical scheduling of concurrent photon streams (implementable). AIS-2: tagged, state-dependent optical routing (requires engineering design). AIS-3: coherent quantum instruction selection (an undefined research hypothesis that must not appear as a working component).

Retired terms

Common mistake

PTEA and TV were earlier working names. They have been replaced by EA and CT; do not use them.

Nothing in this library depends on the earlier project terms above.
