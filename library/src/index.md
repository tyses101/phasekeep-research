# index

This is a free, plain-language book series about one question: **can a single particle of light carry a lot of information, change it on purpose, and let us check that nothing broke along the way?** PhaseKeep is a research proposal that explores that question. This library explains the ideas from the ground up, shows the arithmetic, and says honestly which parts are solid and which are still guesses.

::: key
**What this library is not.** PhaseKeep has not built a quantum computer. No PhaseKeep hardware measurement exists yet. Everything here is established physics, a published result we read, a calculation we ran, a computer model with stated assumptions, or a proposal. Every claim carries a label that says which. You will see the labels everywhere: [[DERIVED]] [[REPORTED]] [[SIMULATED]] [[ASSUMED]] [[PROPOSED]].
:::

## Who it is for

Anyone curious. You do not need quantum computing background. The main text of every volume is written for a reader who knows high-school science. Boxes marked **Go deeper** hold the optional math and the evidence trail for readers who want it, and you can skip every one of them without losing the thread.

## Three ways to read

| If you are... | Read |
|---|---|
| Curious, no physics background | Start Here, then First Words (a plain-language glossary of every background term), then Volumes 1, 2, 3, 4 in order. Skip the Go-deeper boxes. Then Volume 12 (one photon's journey) and Volume 7 for the best story in the project. The FAQ answers the common questions. |
| An engineer or builder | Start Here, Volumes 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, 12. Run the programs in Volume 14. Use Volume 9 and the Ledger (Appendix 2) to check numbers. |
| A researcher or reviewer | Volume 9 first, then Volume 13 for context, the Ledger and Sources (Appendices 2 and 3), the Edition Notes (Appendix 6), then any volume you want to challenge. |
